The average kitchen has more going on than anyone can track in their head.

$1,500+

is what the average household throws out in food each year, not from carelessness, just from forgetting what's in the back of the fridge.

That's not a guilt trip. It's just math nobody has time to run in their head every day.

Ripe isn't asking you to become a zero-waste household overnight. It's just keeping track of what you have, so you can actually use it and eat well while you're at it.

What Ripe does

Everything your kitchen needs to run itself.

One app for what you have, what's about to expire, and what to make with it tonight.

Inventory

Every item, every date, in one place.

Scan a barcode or add items by hand. Track quantities down to the gram or the item, sorted by fridge, freezer, or pantry, so you always know what's actually in the house.

Discover

Recipes that actually use what you have.

Browse and filter by cuisine, time, and difficulty, or let Ripe match recipes against your own pantry, so "what should I make?" has a real answer, not a shrug.

Cook

Clear steps, real ingredient counts, no guesswork.

Every recipe shows prep time, ingredient count, and steps up front. Save the ones you love, rate the ones you've made, and build a personal collection that's actually yours.

How it works

Three habits. That's the whole system.

1

Add what you buy

Scan it or type it in. Takes a few seconds, and it's the only step that takes effort.

2

Get nudged before it's too late

Ripe watches best-before dates and reminds you while there's still time to use something, not after.

3

Cook from what you have

Open the app when you're hungry, not just when you're organizing. Recipes are matched to your actual pantry.

The why

We built Ripe because we kept doing the same thing.

Buy groceries with good intentions. Forget what's in the back of the fridge. Find it three weeks later. Feel a little bad about it. Repeat.

Ripe started as a way to fix that: a simple place to track what's actually in the kitchen. But the more we used it, the more it became about something bigger, making it easier to decide what to eat, plan ahead, and get better at cooking with what's already yours.

Less waste is still a big part of it. But mostly, we just wanted a kitchen that felt a little less chaotic.
